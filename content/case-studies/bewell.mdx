<Section title="Brief">

<div className="grid gap-8 md:grid-cols-[3fr_2fr] items-start">

<div>

BeWell is a mobile app that brings hospital workers to Barnsdall Art Park for a short break: a mindful walk, a breathing exercise, and a journal entry. I worked with three teammates on this 16-week project in Fall 2024. My part covered UX/UI design, UX research, user testing, and making the product video.

The project started with a broad brief about the park and two neighborhoods. Research narrowed it to one group of people with one problem: healthcare workers who are burned out and work a few blocks from a park they rarely use.

<Section title="The starting question">

<div className="grid gap-8 md:grid-cols-[3fr_2fr] items-start">

<div>

Barnsdall Art Park sits on a hill between East Hollywood and Los Feliz. It was built in 1919 as private land and is now a public park run with the Barnsdall Art Park Foundation, whose mission is to keep it a vibrant, inclusive space for creativity and community.

Our brief from that mission was:

</div>

<KeyInsight>

How might we use Barnsdall Park to unite the communities of East Hollywood and Los Feliz?

</KeyInsight>

</div>

</Section>

<Section title="Research">

<p style={{maxWidth:'none', marginTop:'2.5rem'}}>Desktop research showed two very different neighbors. East Hollywood has about 64,000 residents to Los Feliz's 32,000, 9% more families, and a median household income of $32,000 against $63,000. East Hollywood also has both of the area's community parks and all three of its hospitals. Nearly 30% of jobs in East Hollywood are in healthcare.</p>

<p style={{maxWidth:'none'}}>Our first interviews were with people in the park. Even regulars hadn't used much of it.</p>

<Section title="Four concepts">

<p style={{maxWidth:'none', marginTop:'2.5rem'}}>We developed four concepts, each answering the same how-might-we question in a different way. <em>Click a concept's name to see it.</em></p>

- <ConceptPoster title="It's All Fun & Games at Barnsdall" src="/images/case-studies/bewell/concept-poster-rewards.jpg" caption="My concept poster, It's All Fun & Games at Barnsdall: challenges and rewards to get people exploring the park and the neighborhoods around it." /> **(my concept):** a rewards app. Visitors sign up, take on challenges across the park's museum tours, music events, and nearby businesses, and earn points they can redeem, so there's always a reason to try something new.
- <ConceptPoster title="AR n Art (ANA)" src="/images/case-studies/bewell/concept-poster-ana.jpg" caption="Concept poster for AR n Art (ANA): 'Where Virtual Art Meets You.'" moreSrc="/images/case-studies/bewell/concept-ana-storyboard.jpg" moreCaption="ANA's storyboard, made after the poster as the concept developed. - <ConceptPoster title="ARTPARK" src="/images/case-studies/bewell/concept-poster-artpark.jpg" caption="Concept poster for ARTPARK: 'Let your work breathe.'" />: a booking service that brings the communities together through art, with local artists renting display space in the park.
- <ConceptPoster title="Be Well at Barnsdall" src="/images/case-studies/bewell/concept-poster-bewell.jpg" caption="Concept poster for Be Well at Barnsdall: 'Unwind and clear your mind.'" />: a guided mindful walk through the park, a breathing exercise, and a reflection in a digital journal.

<p style={{maxWidth:'none'}}>Be Well fit what our research kept finding: the park was a peaceful refuge that many locals didn't know about, and the people with the most to gain from that kind of refuge worked a few blocks away.</p>

<p style={{maxWidth:'none'}}>Even though my team moved forward with Be Well, the final app pairs Be Well with many features from the other three concept posters. From my poster, <em>It's All Fun & Games at Barnsdall</em>:</p>

- **An account to come back to.** Like Christopher in my poster, you sign up for the app before you start.
- **Challenges.** The home screen offers a daily challenge, and the profile tracks milestone challenges like sharing your first activity.
- **Rewards for showing up.** My poster's points became BeWell's badges, streaks, and milestones.
- **A reason to explore.** My poster used rewards to get people trying new parts of the park. BeWell's badges feature the park's art, and each one opens a page about the artist.
- **Community through activity.** My poster set out to help people find community through what they do at the park. BeWell's Mind Branch is where people share their activities with each other.

<Section title="Narrowing to burnout">

<div className="grid gap-8 md:grid-cols-[3fr_2fr] items-start">

<div>

Choosing Be Well changed who the project was for. Instead of "both neighborhoods," we focused on the people who work closest to the park. Three hospitals sit within half a mile, less than an 11-minute walk: Kaiser Permanente Los Angeles Medical Center, Children's Hospital Los Angeles, and Hollywood Presbyterian Medical Center. Together they employ about 14,000 healthcare workers.

The U.S. Surgeon General describes burnout as emotional exhaustion, cynicism, and a low sense of accomplishment at work. About two-thirds of nurses nationwide experience it. Hospital gardens help: most of their visitors are hospital employees (60%), and 95% of visitors find them therapeutic. None of these three hospitals has a garden. An article in the National Library of Medicine found that mindfulness, stress management, and small group discussion help reduce burnout, all things a park can hold.

<Section title="Competitive analysis">

<p style={{maxWidth:'none', marginTop:'2.5rem'}}>We looked at three apps, each strong at one part of what we wanted:</p>

- **Headspace:** evidence-based meditation and mindfulness.
- **Vitality:** a reward system tied to real-world activity.
- **Insight Timer:** "My Workplace," a way to connect with colleagues.

<p style={{maxWidth:'none'}}>None of them was tied to a place. BeWell would combine all three around one park.</p>

<Section title="The concept">

<p style={{maxWidth:'none', marginTop:'2.5rem'}}>BeWell rests on four ideas:</p>

- **Proximity:** the app only opens its activities once you check in at the park, so it gets you outside instead of keeping you on your phone indoors.
- **Mindfulness:** short guided walks, breathing exercises, and journal prompts, most between 2 and 10 minutes, to fit a hospital break.
- **Gamification:** streaks, milestones, and badges to give people a reason to come back, much like the challenges and rewards in my Fun & Games concept.
- **Community:** the Mind Branch, a shared map where people can choose to post their activity at the park.

<Section title="Usability testing">

<p style={{maxWidth:'none', marginTop:'2.5rem'}}>We tested the prototype by asking people to check in at the park, pick a breathing exercise and add it to the Mind Branch, and write a journal entry. The questions they asked out loud showed us where the design wasn't explaining itself:</p>

<p style={{maxWidth:'none'}}>What we heard, and what we changed:</p>

- **Check-in was unclear.** The small "I'm here!" link became a full-width Check-in button under a map of the park, with a line saying why: checking in unlocks your exercises.
- **No one knew what the Mind Branch was.** Our own prototype called it both "Mind Branch" and "Olive Branch." We settled on one name, gave it its own onboarding slide, and added a note to the breathwork and journal pages explaining what it shows.
- **Journaling had too many steps.** We removed the "Situation" label, put the prompt and the writing area on one screen, and replaced Submit, Archive, and Post with a single Finalize. People who want guidance pick Random Prompt; people who don't pick General.
- **Privacy was a real concern.** Activities are now private by default, and the activity pages say so. Sharing to the park's public Mind Branch is a separate, deliberate step.
- **Exercises didn't say what to expect.** Each session now shows a speaker icon for audio, its length, and a one-line description.
- **Icons needed words.** Every tab now has a label, and the journal has a history button for past entries.
- **Gamification got mixed reactions.** Testers liked tracking progress but worried it could add pressure, and didn't want to be labeled "Beginner." We kept rewards low-key: streaks, milestones, an encouraging empty state, and badges that double as a guide to the park's art.

<p style={{maxWidth:'none'}}>Two suggestions we didn't get to: password-protected journal entries and personalized reminders.</p>

<Section title="Reflection & future considerations">

<p style={{maxWidth:'none', marginTop:'2.5rem'}}><strong>Let the research choose the audience.</strong> Our brief asked how Barnsdall Art Park could unite East Hollywood and Los Feliz, which could have meant anyone. The numbers narrowed it: nearly 30% of local jobs are in healthcare, three hospitals sit within half a mile of the park, and none of them has a garden. I learned to follow that kind of finding, even when it moves a project away from its first idea.</p>

<p style={{maxWidth:'none'}}><strong>Naming is design.</strong> Our prototype called the same feature both "Mind Branch" and "Olive Branch," and testers didn't know what either one meant. The name made sense to us because we had been saying it for weeks. Testers only had the screen. The fix was one name, explained where people first meet it.</p>

<p style={{maxWidth:'none'}}><strong>Rewards can work against a wellness app.</strong> Testers liked seeing their progress but worried it could add pressure, and they didn't want to be labeled "Beginner." An app meant to ease burnout shouldn't grade the people using it. We kept rewards low-key and tied badges to the park's art, so earning one is also a reason to explore.</p>

<p style={{maxWidth:'none', marginTop:'2.5rem'}}>If I kept working on BeWell, I would:</p>

- **Test with more nurses.** We interviewed two, Daniel and Riley, and their input shaped the concept. I'd want more of the people BeWell is for to test it, ideally on a real break at the park.
- **Measure whether it helps.** Our testing showed that people could use BeWell, not that it eases burnout. A pilot with one hospital, with a short stress check before and after, would show whether it works.
- **Finish what testing raised.** Password-protected journal entries and personalized reminders were both suggested and are still open.

<div className="grid gap-8 md:grid-cols-[3fr_2fr] items-start" style={{marginTop:'2.5rem'}}>

<div>

**What it would take to launch.** We closed the project by mapping what BeWell would need to exist outside a prototype:

- **Infrastructure:** the Barnsdall Art Park Foundation would need to invest in building and maintaining the app.
- **Partners:** we proposed partnering with the nearby hospitals to include BeWell in the benefits package for healthcare employees.
- **Reach:** grow BeWell's reach beyond its first users.
