<Section title="Problem">

<div className="grid gap-8 md:grid-cols-[3fr_2fr] items-start">

<div>

Behavioral Intervention Plans (BIPs) are widespread in schools, but are often built on outdated paradigms. BIPs prioritize adult oversight and reactive behavior correction over proactive child engagement. These systems fall short for neurodivergent children who often overlook early signs of distress, resulting in missed windows for early intervention and rising emotional costs.

This disconnect leads to:

- **Increased masking**, emotional burnout, and misinterpreted behaviors
- **Missed or delayed intervention windows** during early signs of distress
- **Fragmented emotional, behavioral, and clinical data** across home, school, and therapy settings
- **Limited caregiver insight** into a child's everyday emotional experiences and needs

With nearly 1 in 7 U.S. students having a learning or regulation difference, and 12% of them on the autism spectrum, the need for better systems is clear. Traditional tools, like Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA) or Individualized Education Programs (IEPs), are often inconsistently applied, or focused more on fixing behavior than fostering internal resilience.

</div>

<KeyInsight>

We address this gap by creating tools that are:

- **Developmentally appropriate:** designed for children's real needs, not adult convenience
- **Socially safe:** empowering children to express and manage their needs without stigma
- **Scientifically grounded:** leveraging cognitive science and embodied cognition
- **System-integrated:** allowing caregivers to track trends, respond early, and coordinate support

</KeyInsight>

</div>

<Section title="Solution">

<div className="grid gap-8 md:grid-cols-[3fr_2fr] items-start">

<div>

UGLOO has two components. The watch sits on the child's wrist, monitors biometric signals, and delivers calm haptic prompts when stress indicators rise, keeping the child in the loop without drawing attention to them. The companion app gives caregivers real-time status, an incident log, and trend data across days and weeks, so they can respond early rather than react after the fact.

By starting from the child's internal cues rather than external disruptions, UGLOO shifts the balance from managing meltdowns to building emotional fluency and independence.

</div>

<Section title="Research">

<div className="my-6 grid gap-8 md:grid-cols-[2fr_3fr] items-start">

<div>

We grounded the project in secondary research on embodied cognition, specifically the 3E (Embodied, Embedded, Enacted) model from Newen, De Bruin & Gallagher, which frames cognition as inseparable from body, environment, and action. For precedents we studied the Sonic Kids Fidget Spinner (engaging but too toy-like for sustained use), the EpiMonitor (strong real-time seizure tracking but no sensory support layer), and the Pebble Watch, whose simple e-paper display and long battery life became a direct reference for UGLOO's watch face.

<p style={{maxWidth:'none'}}>Our primary persona is Milo, a 10-year-old with Level 1 Autism who is highly verbal but struggles with emotional regulation and social interactions. UGLOO is designed to connect all three to Milo's daily emotional experience.</p>

<KeyInsight>

How might we design a complementary system for children (8–13) in Behavioral Intervention Plans, to initiate self-regulation while giving caregivers, educators, and clinicians shared insights?

<Section title="Watch design">

<p style={{maxWidth:'none', marginTop:'2.5rem'}}>The UGLOO wearable monitors the child through five sensors: a PPG sensor (heart rate), an EDA sensor (stress response), a thermistor (body temperature), an IMU (motion and sleep), and GPS. All of them sync to the companion app over Bluetooth Low Energy.</p>
<p style={{maxWidth:'none'}}>We started with hand-drawn sketches exploring round and rectangular form factors, fidget placement, an early panic button concept, and a minimal screen layout.</p>

Christine's feedback named a structural risk: a single fixed fidget would lose its effect for children who stim in varied ways and need variety in stimuli to stay engaged. The interchangeable system addressed this directly: children pick the fidget that matches how they need to regulate in that moment.

</div>

**Incident log**

The incident log surfaces every elevated heart rate event the watch auto-logged, each with a timestamp. <Section title="Future considerations">

<div className="grid gap-8 md:grid-cols-[3fr_2fr] items-start">

<div>

Eight weeks was not enough to close every loop. Three directions we would pursue next:

- **Clinical and educator portals:** extending the incident log to therapists and teachers, so what Ava sees becomes a shared clinical record rather than a private parent view
- **Child-facing biofeedback:** giving Milo himself access to his own pattern data over time, closing the feedback loop the watch starts
- **District-level implementation:** working with legislators and district leaders on California's IDEA law to bring UGLOO into school systems at scale

<Section title="Reflection">

<p style={{maxWidth:'none', marginTop:'2.5rem'}}>Leading UGLOO's brand showed me that visual identity is part of accessibility work, not a layer on top of it. We were designing for children who can be overwhelmed by sensory input, so I chose Lexend for body text to reduce cognitive load and kept the colors sensory-friendly, so the watch and app feel calm instead of alarming.</p>
<p style={{maxWidth:'none'}}>A peer design review changed how I tell this story. Reviewers said we spent too long on specs and process and wanted to meet Milo before the product, so this case study tells his story before showing the watch and app designs. One reviewer also pointed out that the biometrics screens read like a general fitness app, while the incident log felt built for Milo. The most specific feature was the most convincing one.</p>
<p style={{maxWidth:'none'}}>If I did this project again, I would test with children and caregivers directly. Our feedback came from a school psychologist and educators, not the people who would wear and use UGLOO every day.</p>
